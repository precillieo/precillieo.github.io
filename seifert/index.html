For Dr. Alan Seifert

Dear Dr. Seifert,

Thank you for taking me on as an advisee through SINAI4US. We spoke briefly in a breakout room at the Mount Sinai Symposium. Ahead of our first meeting, I want to share my background and the questions I'm working through.

Where I am

I started out in Medical Rehabilitation at Obafemi Awolowo University in Nigeria. In 2022 I moved to Canada to study Computer Science at Carleton University, and since leaving my medical program I have wanted to work at the intersection of AI and medicine. During undergrad I worked with three professors across two research projects: eye-tracking software for concussion assessment, and ultrasound simplification through segmentation, which became a paper at SPIE Medical Imaging 2026 on how much annotation segmentation models need and how task complexity changes that. Alongside school I worked across several engineering teams at Shopify.

After graduating in August 2025 I joined Shopify full-time, building and evaluating large language models. That same year I applied to PhD programs in AI in Medicine, Biomedical Engineering, Biomedical Data Science, and Medical Engineering and Medical Physics, at Harvard, Johns Hopkins, Stanford, MIT, and Cedars-Sinai. I was rejected from all of them. This September I joined Jane, a health-tech company for health and wellness practitioners, as an AI software developer, and I stayed on in Dr. Matthew Holden's lab at Carleton as a research assistant, where I now run a tracked spine ultrasound study for which I obtained ethics approval. I'm reapplying this cycle, and Mount Sinai is my top priority.

What I want from a PhD

From attending conferences, I've formed a few opinions, though I know they may not be entirely right. I want a program that keeps me close to clinical problems and the people who have them while I do AI research. Programs where I'd work with one professor on a narrow area from the start feel too limited for that. I want to rotate through labs, explore different problems, and take a range of courses until I find my path. Since attending SPIE 2026, I've learned about Medical Physics, where I could complete a PhD, do a residency, and go on to become a clinical medical physicist.

My statement of purpose, personal statement, and CV are below. The statement of purpose is what I would commit to; this page is what I'm still unsure about and want to talk through. The faculty paragraph in the statement is unfinished on purpose, pending your advice.

Questions for you

I'd value your perspective on these questions:

  1. Does my read on rotation-based programs versus single-lab programs hold up from the inside?
  2. I've shared my current materials. How can I strengthen them before December 1?
  3. Which AIET or BMEII faculty would you send me to, given my work on label-efficient imaging? Is anyone at Mount Sinai working on space health?
  4. Given my background, are medical physics and AI in medicine both realistic options for me, and how would you weigh them? I'm an international applicant, which I know changes the calculus.
